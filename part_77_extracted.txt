PART 77 — GSIR TRUST, PROVENANCE INTEGRITY, REPRODUCIBILITY AND RESEARCH ASSURANCE ARCHITECTURE

77.1 PURPOSE

The GSIR Research Assurance Layer shall ensure that scientific results, data transformations, AI operations, models, workflows, experiments, evidence and knowledge can be independently inspected, reproduced, verified and trusted.

Scientific data governance increasingly requires lifecycle controls spanning stakeholders, processes and governance elements, while provenance integrity is particularly important for scientific workflows and AI systems.

77.95 ASSURANCE COMPLETION CRITERIA

The GSIR assurance architecture shall be considered complete when:

- Every critical research object has an identity.

- Every important artifact has integrity verification.

- Every critical transformation has provenance.

- Every reproducibility-sensitive execution captures its environment.

- Models have versioned lineage.

- Software dependencies are recorded.

- AI operations have execution provenance.

- Retrieval context can be traced where required.

- Claims have evidence chains.

- Results have computational lineage.

- Reproduction can be executed independently.

- Reproduction failures can be diagnosed.

- Trust can be revoked.

- Downstream impact can be calculated.

- Verification status is explicit.

- Human and automated verification are distinguished.

- Assurance policies are versioned.

- Critical failures stop trust-sensitive operations.

- Audit packages can be generated.

- Assurance records are themselves protected.

77.97 FINAL RESEARCH ASSURANCE PRINCIPLE

GSIR shall never treat a scientific result as trustworthy merely because an AI model, researcher, workflow or database produced it.

Trust shall be established through evidence:

IDENTITY

+

PROVENANCE

+

INTEGRITY

+

EXECUTION CONTEXT

+

VALIDATION

+

REPRODUCIBILITY

+

ATTESTATION

This architecture makes provenance and verification operational rather than documentary, which is particularly important as scientific systems increasingly combine data-intensive computing, AI models, automated workflows and multi-stage research pipelines.
